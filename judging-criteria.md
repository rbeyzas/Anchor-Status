1. Meaningful Idea & Real-World Impact
   Evaluate whether the project solves a meaningful problem with real adoption potential.
   Is the problem statement clear, specific, and well-defined?
   Does the project address a genuine real-world use case (payments, DeFi, RWAs, identity, agentic commerce, remittances, etc.)?
   If successful, who benefits from the solution and what is its potential impact?
   Is there evidence of market viability, including a clear target user or customer segment?
2. Technical Implementation

   Assess the quality and completeness of the technical execution.
   Is the project deployed on Stellar Testnet with working functionality (not mocked or hardcoded)?
   Does the application work reliably from end to end?
   Are Soroban authentication and storage patterns used correctly (instance, persistent, temporary)?
   Is the system architecture appropriate for the problem and clearly documented?
   Scale Track only: Is a Mermaid architecture diagram included and does it accurately represent the system?
   Passkeys or smart wallets are considered a bonus, not a requirement.

3. Ecosystem Fit
   Measure how deeply the project integrates with the Stellar ecosystem.
   Does the project integrate with an eligible Stellar protocol or ecosystem partner?
   Is the integration essential to the product rather than superficial?
   Does the project implement a real Anchor or local payments flow (e.g. TRY ↔ Stellar assets via SEP standards)?
   Does it make effective use of Stellar SDKs, CLI, and Skills resources?
   Are the relevant Stellar Skills references documented in the submission?
   Note: Anchor and local payments integrations are weighted more heavily than the other Ecosystem Fit criteria for both tracks.
4. User Experience
   Evaluate usability and product quality.
   Is the interface intuitive for first-time, non-crypto users?
   Are the implemented features clearly demonstrated within the UI?
   Is the overall product complete, polished, and easy to navigate?
5. Traction & Continuity
   Consider the project's potential beyond the hackathon.
   Did the team validate the product with real users during the event?
   Is there a credible roadmap for post-hackathon development?
   Does the team clearly indicate whether they plan to pursue SCF, InstaAwards, or another continuation path?
   Is the team realistically capable of continuing development after the event?
6. Presentation & Documentation
   Judge how effectively the project is communicated.
   Is the project clearly explained in the demo and README?
   Can judges easily understand the product, architecture, and user flow?
   Do the documentation and testing instructions make the project easy to evaluate?
   Is the submission mature and well-structured enough to be a strong candidate for a future Stellar Community Fund (SCF) or InstaAwards application?
